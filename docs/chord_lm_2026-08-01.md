# A half-bar chord language model (2026-08-01)

**One line:** a transformer that reads a chart as one token per half-bar and
predicts chords the way a language model predicts words — and it works well
enough to be worth wiring into the pipeline.

Branch `feat/chord-lm`, code in `harmonia_min/chord_lm/`.

---

## 1. Does this already exist? No — not at this granularity.

| what | grain | bar timing | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chordonomicon (arXiv 2410.22046), 666k songs, GPT-2 | chord events | **none** | can't answer "does it change this half-bar" |
| `scripts/train_progression_lm.py` (ours, 2026-07-05) | chord events | none | MLP(last-4) ≈ LSTM, 42.6% next-chord |
| `ProgressionEncoder` (ours, issue #21) | chord events | none | shipped, then turned OFF (−3.6pp on the real path) |
| **this** | **half-bar slots** | **yes** | new |

Every prior chord LM — ours and the field's — models the *sequence of chord
changes*. It knows `Dm → G7 → C`, but not that the `Dm` lasted two bars. Our
pipeline runs on a metrical grid and its hardest question is **when** the
harmony changes, not just what it changes to. That question is unanswerable in
an event-level model, which is why this had to be built rather than downloaded.

## 2. The tokens

One token per half-bar. 90 of them:

- **84 chords** = 12 roots × 7 families (`maj min dom dim hdim sus aug`).
  No 7ths — a 7th is an acoustic question, not a grammatical one. Except
  *dominant*, which is a family in its own right, because being a dominant is
  exactly what makes a V a V.
- **`N.C.`** — no chord.
- **`%`** (REP) — same chord as the previous half-bar.
- `PAD/BOS/EOS/MASK`.

`%` is the load-bearing design choice. 44.5% of half-bars are holds, so writing
the chord out again would let a model score 44.5% by pure copying and would
hide the interesting question. Splitting *harmonic rhythm* (change or hold) from
*identity* (change to what) makes both separately measurable — and the tables
below report them separately for exactly that reason.

Autumn Leaves, tokenised:

```
| C:min   %       | F:dom   %       | Bb:maj  %       | Eb:maj  %       |
| A:hdim  %       | D:dom   %       | G:min   %       | %       %       |
```

**Roots are functional, not sounding-bass.** The rest of the project targets the
sounding bass pitch class; a grammar model must not — `D-7/A` and `D-7` have to
be the same token or ii-V-I stops looking like ii-V-I. Anything wiring the LM
into the live pipeline has to convert. (`harmonia_min/chord_lm/vocab.py`.)

## 3. The corpus

`data/ireal/*.txt` — 2,401 iReal charts, 2,172 of them in 4/4, **95,666 bars /
191k half-bar slots**, split by song (never by bar: a tune's A section can't leak
into test through its own repeats).

Two facts that made the half-bar grain the right first choice:

| chords in the bar | share | half-bar grid |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 69.2% | exact |
| 2 | 27.3% | exact |
| 3 | 2.5% | 3rd chord dropped |
| 4+ | 0.9% | 2nd and 4th dropped |

**96.6% of bars survive the grid exactly.** And the vocabulary is essentially
complete: **0.003%** of chord symbols fail to map (4 out of 131,629), and
unmappable ones return `None` rather than silently defaulting to major — the
exact bug `labels.py` was written to prevent.

Two loaders, deliberately asymmetric:

- **iReal** (`corpus.py`) must *reconstruct* within-bar positions — pyRealParser
  drops them, so `G7 Ab7 G7` arrives with no spacing. Rule: chords split the bar
  evenly, remainder to the earlier chords (3 chords in 4/4 → 2+1+1).
- **our own charts** (`from_app.py`) carry an explicit `beat` per chord, so that
  conversion is **exact** — no heuristic.

The LM therefore trains on high-trust chords with reconstructed rhythm, and is
applied to uncertain chords with exact rhythm.

## 4. Control: is longer context all you need?

The obvious objection to "attention wins" is that the transformer merely sees
more history, and an n-gram with the same window would match it. It would not.
Interpolated n-grams, counted in all 12 keys, same splits:

| order | context | ppl | token acc | distinct contexts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.5 bar | 12.13 | 45.8% | 86 |
| 3 | 1 bar | 8.13 | 52.3% | 4,468 |
| 4 | 1.5 bars | 7.79 | 53.8% | 40,052 |
| **5** | **2 bars** | **6.98** | **57.2%** | 121,516 |
| 6 | 2.5 bars | 7.03 | 57.4% | 236,738 |

**N-grams saturate at order 5 and order 6 is worse.** At order 6 the EM-fitted
interpolation weight on the top order collapses to 0.11 — the model itself
learns to ignore its own longest context, because 237k contexts estimated from
1.8M tokens are too sparse to be worth anything. That is the ceiling of counting.
Generalising across similar-but-unseen contexts is what the transformer adds.

## 5. Results

Held-out test songs (217 charts, 17,771 scored half-bars). Columns: `tok%` =
next-slot token accuracy over all 90 tokens; `chg%` = "does the harmony change
here"; `id|chg%` = the right chord *given* that a change happens (argmax over
chord tokens only) — the number that transfers to the pipeline.

| model | ppl | tok% | chg% | id\|chg% | root% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| always say "hold" | — | 45.16 | 45.16 | 3.04 | 13.01 |
| unigram | 15.59 | 45.16 | 45.16 | 6.15 | 13.01 |
| bigram | 12.13 | 45.78 | 46.19 | 17.89 | 30.81 |
| trigram | 8.13 | 52.26 | 63.22 | 30.70 | 43.21 |
| best n-gram (order 5) | 6.98 | 57.22 | 69.18 | 38.39 | 48.74 |
| cloze bigram (both sides) | 9.02 | 49.74 | 56.58 | 25.64 | 38.62 |
| transformer causal, absolute pos | 3.17 | 71.77 | 85.75 | 63.13 | 67.81 |
| transformer causal, **rotary pos** | 2.68 | 76.73 | 87.98 | 69.48 | 73.67 |
| + repetition cache | 2.60 | 78.00 | 88.76 | 70.50 | 74.54 |
| transformer masked (cloze), absolute pos | 2.32 | 77.82 | 89.70 | 69.72 | 73.54 |

**It works.** The best configuration writes the right chord on 78% of half-bars
against a 45% "always hold" floor and a 57% best-n-gram ceiling, and gets the
chord identity right 70% of the time at the moments the harmony actually moves.

Three things had to be fixed to get there, and each was a measurement problem
before it was a modelling one.

### 5a. The masked model was undertrained, not worse

At 80 epochs each, the masked (both-sided) model scored *below* the causal one —
46.3% vs 57.9% identity — despite having strictly more information. That is
impossible if both are converged, so it was a training-budget artifact: the
masked model gets a loss on only the ~16% of slots it masks, the causal model on
every slot. Same wall-clock epochs = ~6× less gradient signal.

At 200 causal / 500 masked epochs the ordering flips to the one information
theory demands: masked 77.82% vs causal 71.77%.

### 5b. The context-length measurement was wrong the first time

Re-scoring each slot with only the last *k* slots visible initially showed short
context doing suspiciously well. Cause: truncating the input left-shifted the
positional embeddings, so a window from bar 30 was told it was at bar 0. The
ablation was measuring a distribution shift, not a loss of context. Fixed by
threading true positions through a new `pos_ix` argument. Corrected:

| visible context | ppl (rotary) | tok% |
|---|---|---|
| 1 bar | 10.54 | 43.91 |
| 4 bars | 5.37 | 60.56 |
| 8 bars | 4.48 | 64.23 |
| 16 bars | 3.54 | 69.83 |
| 32 bars | 2.82 | 75.57 |
| **whole song** | **2.68** | **76.73** |

With absolute positions the curve flattened after 32 bars; with rotary it keeps
climbing to the end of the song.

### 5c. The model was not exploiting repetition — this was the big one

Louis's prediction: *"un LLM qui a comme contexte toute la chanson devrait
facilement refaire les patterns."* Charts are built from literal repeats, so a
model seeing the whole song should nail them.

Test: split test slots by whether their previous 4 bars already occurred
verbatim earlier in the *same* song (35.5% of slots do), and compare against a
rule that just copies whatever followed last time.

| | model | copy rule | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| absolute positions | 72.1% | 91.3% | **19.2pp** |
| rotary positions | **88.0%** | 91.3% | **3.3pp** |

Absolute positions were leaving 19 points on the table. The diagnosis: with a
learned absolute table, "match this pattern somewhere earlier" is a *different*
attention pattern for every offset. Rotary (relative) positions make it one
pattern — the standard prerequisite for induction heads. Swapping them closed
the gap to 3.3pp and also improved novel-context slots (65.7% → 70.5%), so it is
not merely bookkeeping.

An explicit **repetition cache** (`repetition.py`) adds the remainder:

    P(slot) = (1-lambda) * P_LM(slot) + lambda * P_copy(slot)

with `lambda` fitted per match length on validation, and zero wherever nothing
matched. On the absolute-position model it was worth **+8.8pp**; on the rotary
model only **+1.3pp**, and its fitted weight at an 8-bar match drops from 0.90 to
0.50 — a clean confirmation that rotary positions internalised most of the same
signal.

*(A smoothing bug hid this at first: `alpha=0.05` spread over 90 tokens gave a
chord seen once before p=0.19. The cache's top-1 was already 94% — it looked
fine by any argmax metric — but its mass was too flat to transfer, so mixing it
in moved +0.6pp instead of +8.8pp. Caught by printing mean p(true) alongside
top-1. Regression test: `test_cache_is_confident_not_just_correct`.)*

### 5d. What it writes

From a 2-bar minor ii-V prompt, temperature 0.8 (rotary model):

```
| D:hdim  %       | G:dom   %       | C:min   %       | F:dom   %       |
| F:min   %       | Bb:dom  %       | Eb:maj  %       | %       %       |
   ... then this 8-bar phrase repeated verbatim, three more times
```

A descending-fifths chain, and the model repeats its own 8-bar phrase — the
behaviour rotary positions were introduced to buy. From a Bb7 prompt it writes a
blues head (`Bb7 ×2 | Eb7 | Bb7 | Cm F7 | Bb`) and then modulates through
Bbm–Eb7–Ab and Abm–Db7–Gb.

## 6. Existing models, actually run (not just read about)

| model | status | duration tokens? |
|---|---|---|
| `musiclang/musiclang-chord-v2-4k` | loads, runs, 5.6M params, vocab 50 | **none** |
| `floriangardin/chord_model` | weights ship without a tokenizer — not runnable standalone | **none** |
| `patcasso/chords_complete_12x_triad_1perbar` | name promises per-bar; repo has a tokenizer and **no weights** | — |
| Chordonomicon (`ailsntua`) | dataset published; GPT-2 weights not released | dataset has no bar timing |

MusicLang generates `CHORD_CHANGE CHORD_DEGREE__1 … CHORD_CHANGE CHORD_DEGREE__1
… CHORD_CHANGE CHORD_DEGREE__1` — emitting the same chord three times in a row,
which is an event model's only way to express a hold. That is precisely the
pathology the `%` token exists to avoid.

A quantitative head-to-head on the half-bar task was deliberately **not**
attempted: it would require converting our chords into their Roman-numeral
degree-within-tonality format, and jazz's non-diatonic chords force a tonality
change on nearly every bar. The number would measure our converter, not their
model.

## 7. How this could enter the pipeline

Not wired in yet — this document reports the model, not an integration. The
shape it would take:

1. `from_app.py` already converts a live chart to tokens **exactly** (our charts
   carry a beat per chord). That side is done and tested.
2. The masked model gives, for any half-bar, `P(chord | everything else in the
   chart)` without seeing that slot. `scripts/chord_lm_apply.py` prints where it
   confidently disagrees with the pipeline.
3. The conversion that must not be skipped: LM roots are **functional**, the
   pipeline's are **sounding bass**. `D-7/A` is `D:min` to the LM and root A to
   the pipeline.
4. The honest expectation: `id|chg%` is ~70%, so the LM is a *prior*, not an
   oracle. It belongs as a factor in a joint decode alongside the acoustic
   evidence — which is exactly where issue #21's ProgressionEncoder failed when
   it was bolted on as a post-hoc reranker instead (blog post 17). The lesson
   from that failure applies verbatim here: measure it on `infer_chords_v1`, not
   on a proxy harness.

## 8. Honest limits

- **The 2.5% of 3-chord bars lose their third chord.** Not approximated —
  dropped. `slots_per_bar=4` is implemented and untested at scale.
- **3/4 is excluded** (5.8% of tunes). A "half bar" of 1.5 beats would put two
  meanings on one slot index and break the metrical embedding.
- **Pickup bars are treated as full bars.** iReal doesn't mark them reliably and
  the corpus has no beat-level ground truth to check against.
- **Section labels are carried but unused.** Chordonomicon reports that
  structural annotations help next-chord prediction; that's an open extension.
- **Trained on jazz-weighted material** (jazz1460 dominates 2,172 tunes). Pop
  behaviour is inherited from pop400 + brazilian220 only.
