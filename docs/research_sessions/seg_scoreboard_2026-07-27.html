Which songs we lose, and how

Every song in the benchmark, worst first. Each bar is the whole song split into the four shapes: how much of it is a clean slice, how much is chopped up, swallowed or straddling. Seven songs is the entire hand-checked set — small, so read the shape of it, not the third decimal.

All seven together, as we ship today

63% of playing time sits in a slice you could name. The other 37% is split roughly 22% swallowed and 15% straddling.

The same thing as numbers

“Slice length” compares the middle length of our slices with the middle length of the chords really played. Where ours are longer than the truth we swallow chords; where they are shorter we chop them up.

What you are looking at. Seven songs — the whole hand-checked benchmark, nothing held back: Bein' Green, Blue Bossa (record and backing track), Close to You, Every Breath You Take, Georgia on My Mind and Stand by Me. Every block on these pages comes from a real run on 2026-07-27. Where there is no hand-checked reference (one 4.6 s hole in Every Breath You Take) the block is hatched and left out of every number, rather than filled in.

How “how much of this slice is really one chord” is measured. For each slice we cut, we look at what the hand-checked chart holds inside it and take the single chord that occupies the most time. If that chord fills 80% or more, you can name the slice — that is the bar you set. Slices that carry the same name back to back are joined first, because on screen they read as one chord. The second number, “chords kept whole”, works the other way round: for each chord really played, how much of it does our single best slice capture — that is what catches a chord chopped into three.
