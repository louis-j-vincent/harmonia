Implied harmony: what should the chart show when nobody is playing a chord?

You listened to the two worst-scoring slices in the whole benchmark and said the model was right:

“for stand by me also only the bass is present — it is enough to deduce the chord from the general harmony but no chord is being played, same as the blue bossa live bass solo section”

That reframes those regions completely. They were filed as a chord-vs-no-chord bug — the model emits N.C., the reference asserts a chord. They are not a bug. The model is acoustically correct; the reference asserts the implied harmony. So the no_chord_policy brick's +2.10 pp was scoring against a reference that is wrong exactly where the brick fires.

This page does three things: it turns “only the bass is present” into a general automated detector; it lets you hear each candidate behaviour; and it re-measures the +2.10 pp honestly. Short version: the +2.10 pp does not survive — and it does not even reproduce on today’s pipeline.

3. The detector — general, not two songs hand-marked

CQT of the HPSS-harmonic signal, summed over MIDI 55–95 (above an upright bass’s top note), smoothed 2 s, divided by the song’s own median. Below 0.45 for at least 3 s → IMPLIED. The threshold was fixed on your ear-anchors before any accuracy number was computed with it, and has not been moved since. It found Blue Bossa’s contrabass solo at 397–440 s unaided.

The detector and the model’s own N agree on 63% of no-chord time — and 83% on Blue Bossa, where the passage is unambiguous. Two independent instruments (the model never sees this detector) pointing at the same seconds. That is finding #9 confirmed as a measurement rather than an anecdote.

What it does not solve. It normalises on the song’s own median, so a wholly bass-only track would defeat it (it would call the whole thing “stated”). Stand By Me’s texture thickens gradually rather than switching on, so 0.45 flags 11.3 s where 0.60 would flag 23.7 s — deliberately left under-called rather than tuned to the score. It says “no chord is sounding”, never “no chord exists”, and it does not tell silence apart from a bass solo.

6. Is there a safe version of holding?

Holding is fine while the form cannot have moved. So the honest test is not “how many seconds” but “could a chord have gone by”: hold across an implied span only if it is no longer than the song’s own median chord, measured on the parts you can hear.

stand_by_me2.01 s0 / 30.0 s of 11.3 s

Zero spans qualify. Not by luck — structurally: the detector refuses to flag anything under 3 s (to avoid slivers), and a 3 s chord is already slow for this repertoire. So every implied span is, by construction, long enough for the harmony to have moved. The safe variant collapses into doing nothing (+0.0000 everywhere, 0 misleading seconds), while plain holding asserts a chord it cannot know across 58.5 s of 65.8. There is no regime in which holding is defensible here.

7. What would actually fix these regions: continue the form

None of the three offered behaviours is what a musician does over a bass solo. They keep playing the changes — the form goes round again. 8. Verdict

The +2.10 pp was an artefact — twice over. Do not wire it.

It does not reproduce. Re-measured today with a bit-identical OFF control, the same brick is worth +0.98 pp root / +0.10 pp partial, not +2.10 / +1.67. Blue Bossa reproduces exactly; Stand By Me does not, and the log says why: suppressing N.C. turns 41 chart cells into 87, which drops the OCCAM loop-family coverage from 0.98 to 0.97 and the simplicity post-pass rejects its own gate — so the flicker survives into the chart. The brick now fights the Occam pass, i.e. it fights the simplicity principle.

And what remains scores for the wrong reason. On the repaired reference it is −0.44 pp on the headline metric. Every positive number it ever had lived in seconds where the reference asserts a chord that nobody plays.

Ship the detector. Mark the region — do not fill it.

The detector is the real deliverable: it is the general automation of your ear-correction, it agrees with the model’s own N.C. on 63% of no-chord time (83% on Blue Bossa) as an independent witness, and any future form-inference work needs it to know where to fire. It is now harmonia/models/harmonic_texture.py.

The recommended behaviour is (c)’s structure and NOT its filling: mark the region implied and invent nothing. Keep the cell as N.C. but change what it means and how it renders — from “nothing here” to “the changes continue, we cannot hear them”. It is the only candidate that is +0.0000 on every reference (it rewrites no label, so there is nothing to regress), has 0 misleading seconds, and is still strictly more informative than today — because it separates genuinely chordless from implied: Blue Bossa’s solo is 83% agreed implied, Georgia’s 4.2 s of N.C. is 0% implied, i.e. really chordless.

This is a UI-honesty feature, not an accuracy brick. Filling the region correctly needs the form model in §7. Default OFF, not wired.
