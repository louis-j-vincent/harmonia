**[OURS]** = measured here. Harness: `scripts/bibar_prep.py` → **285 McGill
Billboard tracks, 2781 annotated section starts**, the annotators' own bar grid
and own NNLS chroma. Placement metric = argmax the cue over a ±4-bar window
around each GT start, **ties broken AT RANDOM** (the ★★ RETRACTED entry in

## (1) Runs AND peaks together — **MEASURED TRUE**

> « les runs de tuilage ne marchent pas bien, et les pics recouvrent très bien,
> ils devraient être pris en compte pour corriger les tuilages, pas l'un ou
> l'autre mais les deux ensemble. »

Measured two ways, both agree.

**Placement.** runs alone 35.8%, peaks alone 30.1%, **summed 41.8%** — the sum
beats the better of the two by 6.0 pp. **[OURS]**

**Boundary detection** (F, bar tolerance, unconditioned): **[OURS]**

| tolerance | run edges only | peaks only | **union** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±0 bar | 0.232 | 0.217 | **0.281** |
| ±1 bar | 0.345 | 0.313 | **0.398** |
| ±2 bar | 0.447 | 0.436 | **0.511** |

Today `detect_sections` picks ONE mode (run edges when coverage ≥ 50%, else
novelty) and, where runs cover, *deletes* the novelty candidates
(`cand = [h for h in cand if not covered[...]]`, sections.py:264). That deleted
set is worth +4.9 pp of boundary F. This is the single change with the clearest
evidence behind it.

## (2) The minimal-loop rule — **premise TRUE, rule NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE**

> « il faut repérer la boucle de répétition minimale au sein de chaque carré de
> tuilage, et un carré est forcément un multiple de cette boucle. »

**Louis is right about the facts.** On 285 tracks, an *annotated* section's
length is a multiple of the minimal loop detected inside it **84.6%** of the
time (1312/1550); our own v1 runs satisfy the same rule only **59.6%**
(884/1484). **[OURS]** His hand count on our 6 charts (22/40 = 55%) reproduces
at corpus scale. The rule describes the ground truth and our code violates it.

**Applying it does not help.** Snapping each square to a multiple of its own
loop, with the phase chosen by the novelty/change peaks, moves 49% of runs and
*lowers* boundary F: 0.232 → **0.213** (±0), 0.345 → **0.328** (±1), 0.447 →
0.448 (±2). **[OURS]** As a placement prior it is at best neutral and
monotonically worse as its weight grows: 41.8% (w=0) → 41.4 (0.25) → 41.2 (0.5)
→ 40.7 (1.0) → 38.8 (2.0).

**Why — and this is the interesting part.** The loop phase is *real signal*: an
annotated section start lands on our detected loop phase **62.5%** of the time
vs **42.1%** by chance (chance = mean 1/L over the detected loops; L=2 for
1047 starts, L=4 for 347, L=8 for 52, L=16 for 24). **[OURS]** But the
information is already inside the lag-runs: with an **ORACLE phase** read off
the song's own first GT start, the constrained cue reaches only 40.8% — *below*
the 41.8% with no constraint at all. The constraint restates, more coarsely,
what the runs already say, and its coarseness costs more than its extra
evidence adds. Most detected loops are 2 bars, where it eliminates only one
candidate in two.

**Not disproven:** the rule as a *length* sanity check when writing the chart
(a 7-bar section with a 2-bar loop is almost certainly a writing error even if
fixing it does not move the start). That is a different metric and was not
measured here.

## (3) Drop the gaussian blur — **TRUE, but not by the stated mechanism**

> « le flou gaussien nous empêche de bien distinguer deux sections qui sont
> similaires mais différentes. »

**(a) Placement — the blur clearly costs.** Novelty alone 27.6% blurred →
**30.1%** un-blurred (+2.5 pp). In the fusion, 40.2% → **41.8%** (+1.6 pp).
**[OURS]** Half of today's total gain comes from deleting one constant.

**(b) Letters — the stated mechanism is NOT supported.** AUC separating GT
same-letter section pairs from different-letter pairs, on `sections.py`'s own
cross/self block ratio: **[OURS]**

| BLUR_SIGMA | letter AUC (n=278 songs) |
|---|---|
| 0.0 | **0.893** |
| 0.5 | 0.891 |
| 1.0 | 0.889 |
| 1.5 (shipped) | 0.886 |
| 2.5 | 0.877 |

Monotone in the predicted direction, but the effect is **+0.007** from σ=1.5 to
0 — the blur does not destroy the same-vs-different distinction. It smears the
*position* of the novelty peak, and that is where it costs. **Right call, wrong
cause** — so use σ=0 in both places (it never hurts), but do not expect it to
fix a letter-assignment problem.

## (4) Distance-aware similarity — **MEASURED FALSE**

> « en général plus on s'éloigne dans le temps entre les répétitions, moins
> elles se ressemblent. »

**Premise, measured on real repeats** (same-position bars of two same-letter GT
sections, binned by how far apart the two occurrences are): **[OURS]**

| distance between the repeats | mean cos | n |
|---|---|---|
| 4–7 bars | 0.892 | 302 |
| 8–15 | 0.896 | 2852 |
| 16–31 | 0.887 | 10410 |
| 32–63 | 0.874 | 14077 |
| 64–127 | 0.862 | 7155 |
| 128–255 | 0.869 | 1099 |

The decay is real but **tiny — 0.034 of cosine over a 16× range, and it turns
back up at the tail.** More decisive: `TILE_MIN = 0.80` sits far *below* every
one of these means, so it is not the thing cutting off distant repeats; there
is nothing for a relaxation to recover.

**And relaxing it loses.** With a floor `0.80 − β·log2(lag/2)`: β=0 → 37.9%,
β=0.02 → 37.5%, β=0.04 → 37.3%, β=0.08 → 37.0%. Moving the base instead is also
flat-to-worse (base 0.70 → 38.8%, base 0.85 → 38.3%, vs 37.9% at 0.80).
**[OURS]**

## What I would change in `harmonia_min/sections.py`

1. **`BLUR_SIGMA = 1.5` → `0.0`** for the novelty path (keep or drop it for the
   letter ratio — it is worth +0.007 AUC there, so dropping is fine too).
2. **Replace the mode switch with a sum.** Stop deleting novelty candidates
   inside run coverage (line 264) and stop the `coverage >= RUN_COVERAGE_MIN`
   either/or; take the union of run edges and peaks, or z-sum the two scores.
3. **Do not implement the minimal-loop snap or the lag-relaxed floor** as
   placement/boundary logic — both measured negative here.

Combined effect of 1+2 on the harness: **27.6% → 41.8% exact-bar** (chance
10.8%). Note the caveat that carries over from yesterday: this is a
**placement/refinement** family, and its unconditioned boundary F (0.281 at ±0)
is still low in absolute terms.
