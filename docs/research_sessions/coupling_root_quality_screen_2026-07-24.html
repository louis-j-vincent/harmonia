Verdict — coupling is NEITHER a root lever nor a quality lever here; it is a uniform regression. Letting the trained head's top-2 root win, decoded jointly with quality, drops pooled root −11.3pp (0.7367→0.6242) and regresses majmin, sevenths, partial and strict by 8–12pp too. No per-song improves. The lever is dead on the shipped nnls24 path and should not be wired in.
Why (root cause). The "coupling" collapses to the known-dead trust-head-root-over-musx re-vote: the coupled argmax equals the pure head-root argmax 94.8% of the time, because the quality-head peak is nearly constant across candidate roots (0.668 at the musx root vs 0.672 at the head root — ~1 nat of range, swamped by the head-posterior top-1/top-2 gap). And on this real-audio benchmark the trained head root is 13.4pp worse than musx (segment root acc 0.675 vs 0.809) — the RWC/Billboard-NNLS domain gap the brief warned about, now quantified. Substituting a worse root can only lose.

What this rules out (and what it does not)

  • Ruled out: re-enabling joint_decode's coupled decode at the _label_segments labeling point on the shipped nnls24 lane. It regresses every metric; do not wire it. No default-OFF brick is warranted.
  • Confirmed caveat (as the brief anticipated): the trained NNLS root/quality heads are usable to decide WHERE chords change (segmentation), but their absolute root/quality scores are too domain-shifted (RWC/Billboard-NNLS → pop/jazz real audio) to overrule musx on WHAT the chord is.
  • Not tested / out of scope: a coupling driven by a head re-trained or calibrated on real-audio chroma, or a candidate set from a source other than the head top-2 (which misses GT on 43% of the target ±P5 errors). The lever fails with today's heads; it is a head-quality problem, not a decode-formulation problem.
