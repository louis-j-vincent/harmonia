1 · The premise screen: is the reference really a metronome?

Yes, and it is not close. Fitting one constant tempo to each song's reference onsets leaves a residual of 0.0–0.3 ms on four of seven songs — including Georgia On My Mind, a rubato ballad no human plays that way. A real recording cannot do that. The other three sit on a piecewise version of the same lattice.

The independent check: does each grid land where the audio actually has note onsets? Beat This! beats score 2.4–4.7× chance; the reference lattice scores at chance. The reference’s onsets carry essentially no information about where the beats are.

2 · Why re-timing the reference did not help

The model quantises its chord boundaries onto its own rigid constant-tempo grid — median distance 0.2 ms. To the real detected beats: 60–104 ms.

So both sides were metronomic. Moving only the reference onto real beats breaks an agreement between two synthetic clocks, and the score goes down. Move both and the score is identical to where it started — a clock is a shared coordinate system, and this one cancels:

The consequence is not that the lattice is harmless — it is that the fix belongs in the model (beat_period_mode: one rigid period for a whole song), not only in the benchmark, and both must move together or the number lies.

Two related tests, both cheap and both negative for the « it’s just shifted » reading: the best possible constant time shift, chosen per song with an oracle, buys only +0.5 pp. But blanking a ±0.25 s collar around every reference boundary buys +5.3 pp — the error is concentrated at boundaries, it is just not a uniform shift.

6 · The STATED / IMPLIED detector

Not hand-marked. It is a general measurement: the CQT of the harmonic (percussion-removed) signal, summed over MIDI 55–95 — everything above the bass register — smoothed over 2 s and divided by the song’s own median. Below 0.45 the mid register has gone quiet: bass only, nothing states the chord.

7 · What I do not want you to read into this

The +3.7 pp is mostly deletion, not vindication. The single biggest term (+3.4 pp) is dropping the 12% of time your ear called wrong — and those regions were selected because the model disagreed with the reference there. Removing them mechanically raises the score. It is evidence the reference is bad, not evidence the model is good.

I did not repair those labels to the model’s answer. You said « le modèle a raison », and if I had taken that literally the score there would be ~1.0 by construction — scoring the model against itself. They are marked unscoreable instead. The true number in those regions is somewhere between 0.64 and 0.68; only a re-transcribed reference can say where.

240 s of 1654 s is now unscoreable (14.5%). That is the honest cost, stated rather than hidden.

So: are we better than we thought? Yes, but by ~4 points, not by a transformation. And the biggest thing this exercise found is not a point gain at all — it is that our own boundaries are on a metronome.
